AOK-ImageTool Guide

A fast, keyboard-first image viewer and editor for macOS, with Apple's on-device intelligence built in.

Getting around

  • Open pictures. File › Open… ⌘O takes images or a whole folder; you can also drop them on the window or the Dock icon. Opening one image shows the rest of its folder too, so you can browse on from it.
  • Browse with the arrow keys. ← → step through the folder; so do ↑ ↓ (or they zoom, if you choose that in Settings), Space and ⇧Space. Home and End jump to the first and last image. When the whole image is visible, a trackpad swipe or the scroll wheel steps through images too.
  • Fast. The images on either side of the one you're looking at are decoded ahead of time, in the direction you're going. Holding an arrow key flips through thumbnails and decodes the full image when you stop. RAW files show their built-in preview first.
  • The sidebar (toolbar button, top left) shows the current folder, its enclosing folder and subfolders, your recent folders, and common places such as Pictures and Downloads. Right-click a folder for Show in Finder or Open in New Window.
  • Folders. Go › Enclosing Folder ⌘↑. Go › Next Folder and Previous Folder (⌥⌘→ ⌥⌘←) walk the folders beside this one, which is handy for date-named photo folders. View › Include Subfolders shows everything below the folder as one list.
  • Each folder remembers the last image you looked at, and opens there next time. File › Open Recent lists recent folders.
  • Windows. ⌘N opens another window, for comparing two folders side by side.

Viewing

  • Formats. Everything macOS can read: JPEG, PNG, HEIC, AVIF, JPEG XL, WebP, TIFF, GIF, BMP, PSD, OpenEXR and 30+ camera RAW formats, plus PDF and SVG. Settings › General lists them all.
  • Zoom. Z or a double-click toggles between fitting the window and actual size; + − zoom, 0 fits, 1 is 100%. Pinch, or hold ⌘ or ⌥ and scroll, to zoom at the pointer. Drag to pan.
  • Lock Zoom (L) keeps the same zoom and position as you move between images: the quickest way to compare focus across a burst of shots.
  • Small images. View › Enlarge Small Images to Fit Window ⌥⌘9 scales them up. Sharpen Enlarged Images with AI shows them through Apple's on-device upscaler instead of plain smoothing.
  • HDR. HDR photos, like those from iPhone, show their full brightness on displays that can show it. A small HDR badge says so.
  • Animations and pages. Animated GIF, PNG, WebP and HEIC play. Multi-page TIFFs and PDFs have page buttons; Page Up and Page Down turn pages.
  • Live Text. Select and copy text right on the image, and click the Look Up button for plants, pets, landmarks and more.
  • Grid (G, or ⌘2) shows the folder as thumbnails; the slider in the toolbar sets their size. Return or a double-click opens the selected image. ⌘1 goes back to the viewer.
  • Filmstrip (T), full screen (F) and slideshow (S; Space pauses it, Esc stops it). The time per slide is in Settings.
  • Info (I) shows a histogram, the camera and lens, exposure, dates, size and color details, a map for photos that know where they were taken, and every bit of metadata under All Metadata. At the bottom, the folder's totals: how many images, picks and rejects.
  • Canvas background. View › Background chooses black, dark gray, gray, light gray, white or the window color. Transparent images show over a checkerboard.

Picking the keepers

  • Flag as you go. P picks, X rejects, U unflags, and the next image comes up (you can turn that off in Settings). Flags are Finder color labels, green and red, so Finder and other apps see them too.
  • Filter. The Sort & Filter button in the toolbar (or View › Show) shows only picks, only rejects or only unflagged images.
  • Move Rejects to Trash (Image › Flag, or the Info panel) clears them out in one go. Moving to the Trash can be undone.
  • Sort by name, date taken, date created or modified, size, kind, at random, or Best Shots: Apple's on-device judgment of how good each photo is. Reverse Order flips any of them.
  • Select several in the grid with ⇧-click, ⌘-click or ⇧ and the arrow keys. Flagging, moving, copying, trashing, sharing and exporting work on the whole selection.
  • Duplicates. Intelligence › Find Duplicates… groups identical and near-identical shots, best first. Slide toward Loose to catch similar shots too, then Keep Best of Each Group marks the rest for the Trash.
  • Find Similar Images (Intelligence menu) shows the images that look like the one you're on; Show All goes back.

Copies and stacks

Black & White, Colorize, Upscale with AI, Extend, Remove Objects, Reimagine and Export (into the same folder) save a copy and leave the original alone. Each copy stacks with the photo it was made from, so a folder doesn't fill up with near-identical pictures.

  • A stack takes one place in the filmstrip and the grid, showing its newest copy on top. Lines above the thumbnail and a count in its corner mark it.
  • Step through a stack with ⌥← and ⌥→ (or Go › Newer Copy in Stack and Older Copy in Stack): newest first, the original last. The window's title says where you are, such as “Stack 2/3”.
  • Spread it out. Click the count, or choose Show All Copies in Stack (Go menu, or right-click), to see every copy side by side; click the count again to stack them up. The stack then shows the copy you were on.
  • Copies of copies (a colorized photo, then upscaled) stay in the original's stack. Renaming the original keeps its copies with it.
  • View › Stack Copies with Originals turns stacking off, showing every file on its own.
  • Changing the image itself instead. In Settings › Editing › Copies, turn off “makes a copy” for Upscale with AI, Colorize, Black & White, Extend or Remove Objects, and its result replaces the image, as Save would: the original goes to the Trash (if Save does that), after asking first (if Save asks). Photos from your Photos library and animations always get a copy.

A copy remembers what it was made from in the file's extended attributes; copies with names like “Beach (Colorized)” or “Beach (2×)” stack even without that.

Searching

Type in the search field in the toolbar. It matches file names, and, once the folder has been analyzed, what's in the pictures (“owl”, “beach”, “receipt”) and text inside them.

  • Analyzing happens on this Mac, the first time you search a folder or when you choose Intelligence › Analyze Folder for Search. A progress ring in the toolbar shows how far it's got. Each folder is analyzed once and remembered.
  • Related words count. If nothing matches exactly, close words are tried: “puppy” finds dogs.
  • Keywords from a description (see Apple Intelligence) are clickable: a click searches the folder for that word.
  • Ending a search: clear the field, or press Esc with the image focused. Return in the field takes you back to the image.

Editing

Edits never touch the file until you Save. Every change can be undone (⌘Z), and unsaved edits are still there after you quit and reopen the app.

  • Adjust (E, or the toolbar button) has Straighten (an angle slider, buttons that turn the picture 1° or 0.1° at a time, and Auto; the corners it turns out of the frame are cropped away), Light (exposure, brightness, contrast, highlights, shadows), Color (saturation, vibrance, warmth, tint), Detail (sharpness, noise reduction, vignette) and 13 filters, each previewed on your photo. Double-click a slider's name to reset it.
  • Black & White… (Image › Black & White…, or Copies at the bottom of Adjust) makes a black and white copy, the darkroom way, and saves it next to the original as “… (Black & White)”. A filter decides which colors turn light or dark: Red makes blue skies dark and dramatic and smooths skin, Yellow is milder, Green separates foliage, Blue brightens skies and darkens skin. Toning (sepia, warm or cool) tints the grays, as much as you like. The preview shows the result before you save; the copy includes your other edits, and remembers the filter you chose for next time.
  • Colorize (Image › Colorize, Copies in Adjust, or the Intelligence panel) turns a black and white photo to color and saves the result next to it, as “… (Colorized)”. The colors come from DDColor, a model made for colorizing that runs on this Mac; the first time, the app offers to download it (about 425 MB, see the models). All the light and detail are the original's, so faces and textures stay exactly as they were. The colors are a well-informed guess: a sweater may come out a different color than it was.
  • Auto enhances the photo in one click (⌥⌘E). Click it again to take it off.
  • Before and after. Click the eye button, or press \, to see the original; again to go back.
  • Rotate and flip. R and ⇧R rotate (also ] [, ⌘R ⌘L); flips are in the Image menu.
  • Crop and straighten (C or ⌘K). Drag the corners and edges, or pick an aspect (Square, 16:9, 4:3…; the button beside it swaps portrait and landscape). The slider straightens (as does Straighten in Adjust); Auto levels it for you, from the horizon or from upright lines such as walls and door frames. Smart Crop frames the subject. Return applies, Esc cancels.
  • Straighten Document (Intelligence menu) finds a page, sign or whiteboard and squares it up.
  • Revert to Original (File menu) drops every edit; Reset Adjustments in the panel keeps the crop and drops the rest.

Extend and Generate

Extend makes a photo bigger than it was: wider for a 16:9 screen, taller for a phone wallpaper, or just with more room around the subject. Your original pixels stay exactly as they were; only the new area is made up.

  1. Intelligence › Extend… ⇧⌘K opens the Crop tool with Extend turned on, using the fill you chose last. (Or press C and click Extend in the bar at the bottom, or click Generative Extend in the Intelligence panel.)
  2. Drag the frame's edges past the photo, up to about a third of its size on each side, or choose an Aspect such as 16:9 to widen it to that shape.
  3. Choose a Fill (Extend starts with the one you used last; Generate, once its model is installed):
    • Match Photo continues the photo's own texture by copying it: skies, grass, sand, water, plain walls. It takes a moment and works on any Mac, but it can't invent objects or structure, so tiles, furniture and people beside the edge come out scrambled; use Generate for those.
    • Generate draws new content that continues the scene, with an image model that runs on this Mac. Apple Intelligence describes the photo's setting (“a restaurant with wooden floors, tables and chairs”, never the people in it), and Generate continues that; the description field shows what it's using. To ask for something else, type it (“sandy beach and blue sky”) and press Return. It needs Apple silicon and the generative fill model, a one-time download: click Get the Model for Generate… in the bar. Without Apple Intelligence it continues the scene from the picture alone.
  4. Look at the preview. Match Photo previews right away. Generate takes about 15–25 seconds, with its progress at the top. Not quite right? Try Again (at the top, or in the bar at the bottom) makes a different one.
  5. Done saves the extended photo as a copy, “name (Extended)”, which stacks on the original; the original stays as it was. The final fill is made first (“Extending…” at the top right, with its progress). For Generate, a second pass redraws the new area at higher resolution and blends it into the photo's edges, which takes about another half minute.

To extend the photo itself instead, turn off Extend makes a copy in Settings › Editing. Done then keeps the extension as an edit of the photo, made in the background (Save waits until it's done), and the rest of this list applies to it.

  • Adjustments apply to the new area too, so you can extend first and adjust later.
  • Rotating, straightening or a bigger frame make a new fill; a smaller frame inside the extended area reuses the one you have. The same edits always make the same fill again, even after a relaunch.
  • Intelligence › Try Another Extend Fill fills the same frame differently without opening the Crop tool. Undo goes back to the previous fill.
  • Scans of prints. If the photo has a print's white (or black) border, Extend continues the picture inside it, and the border, with any date stamp on it, is replaced by the extended scene.
  • Photos with see-through areas can't be extended until they have a background (see below), since there's nothing to continue.

Generated content is new, not recovered: it's the model's idea of what might be there. It can add things, such as another flower or something on the floor, so check the result before you share it, and try again if it isn't right. The bigger the frame, the more it has to make up: modest extensions look most natural. On a big photo the new area is a little softer than the original.

Removing objects

Intelligence › Remove Objects… ⇧⌘X (or Remove Objects… in the Intelligence panel) takes things out of a photo: a passer-by, a sign, a power line, a stain. What was behind them is filled in from what's around them.

  1. Mark what to remove. Paint over it ([ and ], or the slider, size the brush), click it to take the whole object (a person, a pet, a car…), or circle it to take everything inside the circle. Cover it generously, with its shadow or reflection if it has one.
  2. It's removed as soon as you let go, as one step. Mark more to remove more. ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z step back and forward; Try Again removes the last one again, filled differently (and with the Fill chosen now); Original compares with the picture before.
  3. Done saves the result as a copy, “name (Retouched)”, which stacks on the original; the original stays as it was. With Remove Objects makes a copy turned off in Settings › Editing, the removals become an edit of the photo instead (non-destructive, undoable, saved with Save).

Choose the Fill in the bar:

  • Erase (best for most things) paints in what was behind with LaMa, a model made for removing objects that runs on this Mac in about a second. It needs Apple silicon and the object removal model, a one-time download: click Get the Model for Erase… in the bar.
  • Match Photo copies the photo's own texture into the gap, like Extend's Match Photo. It works on any Mac without a download and is very good on grass, sand, sky, water and walls, less so where there's structure (a building, a fence).
  • Generate replaces rather than removes: type what to put there (“tall grass”, “a wooden bench”) and the generative fill model paints it in, in the photo's light. (Asked for nothing, a model like this paints a new object into the gap, so it isn't offered for plain removal.)

Removals are made on the photo itself, so cropping, turning, extending or adjusting it afterwards keeps them. On a Live Photo they make it a still, as Extend does.

Subjects and backgrounds

  • Remove Background (⌥⌘B, or in the Intelligence panel) keeps the subject: a person, pet, object or several. Choose it again to restore the background.
  • Select Object… (Intelligence menu) lets you pick exactly what to keep. Click an object to select it, click more to add, ⌥-click to leave something out. Then Cut Out (keeps only the selection, still undoable), Copy, or Save as PNG….
  • Copy Subject ⇧⌘C puts just the subject on the clipboard, ready to paste into Keynote, Messages or a document.
  • A new background. With the background removed, or for a picture that already has transparency (a logo, a saved cutout), choose what goes behind it in the Intelligence panel or Intelligence › Background: transparent, a color, the original blurred (a portrait look), a picture of your own, or a generated one:
    • Generate on This Mac… (with the generative fill model) paints the scene around your subject, so its framing, eye level and light suit them. Describe the scene (“a sunny beach with palm trees”, or pick a suggestion); Apple Intelligence describes the subject (“a smiling woman with grey hair and a blue shirt”), which you can correct, and both go to the model. Try Again makes another; Use Background puts it behind the cutout.
    • Generate with Image Playground… makes a stand-alone picture from a few words, in Image Playground's styles.
  • Match Lighting (with a picture background) blends the subject's brightness, light color and edges with the new scene, so it looks like it was there. The slider sets how much.

Apple Intelligence

Everything here runs on this Mac. The Intelligence panel (A, or ⌘J) gathers it; most of it is also in the Intelligence menu.

  • Describe Image ⇧⌘D writes a title, an alt-text caption and keywords. Copy Alt Text for a website or document; Add Finder Tags makes the keywords Finder tags, which Spotlight finds; Rename to “…” takes the suggested file name. Settings can describe each image automatically while the panel is open.
  • Ask About This Image. Type a question (“What breed is this?”, “Where could this be?”) or click a suggestion. Follow-up questions remember the conversation.
  • Text in Image. Read Text finds all the text in a photo (signs, receipts, slides) to copy or Translate….
  • Smart Fixes: Auto Enhance, Straighten (levels the horizon or upright lines), Smart Crop, Fix Document, and, with their models installed, Generative Extend and Colorize.
  • Reimagine in Image Playground… turns the photo into an animation, illustration or sketch, saved next to the original and stacked with it.
  • Upscale with AI (Image menu, or Copies in Adjust: 2×, 3× or 4×) saves a sharper, larger copy, such as “Fox (4×).heic”, next to the original and stacked with it. Images larger than 1920 × 1920 are upscaled in overlapping tiles; the result can be up to 16,384 pixels on a side. macOS downloads Apple's upscaling model the first time. Animations (GIF, animated PNG, WebP or HEIC) are upscaled frame by frame, keeping their timing and looping, and saved as a GIF, or as an animated PNG in full color: choose under Save Animation As in the Upscale menu. GIFs have 256 colors a frame, so some of the new detail turns into fine dithering, and files grow quickly: 2× is usually plenty. For an animation the menu shows how big each scale would be, and leaves out any that would be too large (over about 35 MB as GIF, 45 MB as animated PNG, which is about four times bigger than GIF for the same animation). Turns, crops and color adjustments apply to every frame; a removed background or Extend doesn't.
  • Shot Quality scores the photo; Best Shots sorts the folder by that score (after Analyze Folder). In a folder you've analyzed, pictures added later, such as copies you make, are scored by themselves, so a stack ranks by its newest copy.
  • This Folder: Analyze Folder (for search), Best Shots, Find Similar and Duplicates.
  • Rename… ⌥⌘R (File menu) has Suggest a Name, from what's in the picture.

Describe, Ask and naming need Apple Intelligence turned on, on a Mac that has it (macOS 26 or later). Image Playground needs it too. Everything else in the panel uses Vision, which every Mac has. See Which macOS has what.

Saving, exporting and files

  • Save ⌘S writes your edits into the file, in the same format, keeping its metadata. Before it does, it asks (you can turn that off), and the original goes to the Trash, so you can always get it back. If the format can't be written (RAW, PDF, or JPEG with see-through areas), use Export.
  • Export… ⇧⌘E saves a copy as JPEG, HEIC, AVIF (on macOS 26 and later), PNG, TIFF, GIF, BMP or JPEG 2000, with a quality setting and an optional size limit. Metadata: keep everything, remove the location (good for posting online), or remove it all. With several images selected in the grid, they're all exported at once.
  • Unsaved edits survive quitting. The app tells you how many are waiting when you quit, and the image shows an “Edited” badge.
  • File menu: Rename… (⌥⌘R), Move To…, Copy To…, Show in Finder (⇧⌘R), Set as Desktop Picture, Print… (⌘P) and Move to Trash (⌘⌫, undoable).
  • Edit menu: Copy Image (with your edits), Copy Subject, Copy File Path. Share is in the toolbar.
  • Paste a picture with ⌘V (or Edit › Paste as New Image) while the image or grid has the focus: a screenshot, a picture copied from a web page or another app becomes a new file in the folder, named like “Pasted Image 2026-10-23 at 09.41.12”. A copied JPEG or HEIC is kept exactly as it was; anything else is saved as PNG, so transparency is kept. Image files copied in Finder are copied in under their own names. With no folder open, or a photo from Photos showing, pasted pictures go to your Pictures folder and open there.

Editing photos from Photos

AOK-ImageTool can edit the photos in your Photos library. Edits are saved as Photos edits: Revert to Original in Photos undoes them, and iCloud Photos carries them to your other devices.

  • File › Open from Photos… ⇧⌘O picks photos in your library, one or many, to browse with the arrow keys and edit as usual. The first time, macOS asks whether AOK-ImageTool may use your Photos library. Originals kept in iCloud are downloaded as they're needed. The title says From Photos, or Live Photo from Photos.
  • Save ⌘S sends the edit to Photos.
  • Live Photos stay live. Light, color, filters, crop, rotation and straightening apply to every frame of the motion too. A removed background and Extend are for a single picture, so a Live Photo with those is saved as a still; you're asked first.
  • Edit again later. A photo you edited here opens with its settings, so you can carry on adjusting instead of starting over. Take every edit off (File › Revert to Original) and save, and it's back to its original in Photos too.
  • Copies you make (Upscale with AI, Colorize, Black & White) are added to your Photos library as new photos.
  • Edits not yet saved to Photos are lost when you quit (edits to files are kept). Rename, move or delete the photos themselves in Photos.
  • Image › Edit With › AOK-ImageTool in Photos works too, for one photo at a time: Save sends the edit back. Photos turns off a Live Photo's motion when it hands it to another app this way, so use Open from Photos for Live Photos.

Settings

AOK-ImageTool › Settings… (⌘,):

  • General: what ↑ ↓ do (browse or zoom); whether Space shows the next image; wrapping around at the end of a folder; moving on after a pick or reject; including subfolders and hidden files; slideshow timing; checking for updates; and the list of supported formats.
  • Viewing: the canvas background, the checkerboard behind transparency, enlarging small images, crisp pixels when zoomed in, HDR, and file names under thumbnails.
  • Editing: moving the original to the Trash on Save, asking before saving over it, whether Upscale with AI, Colorize, Black & White, Extend and Remove Objects each make a copy or change the image itself, and the image models (generative fill, colorizing, object removal): their status, Download, Remove…, and under More, installing one you converted yourself and the model's license.
  • Intelligence: whether Apple Intelligence is available, describing images automatically, Live Text, and Clear Cached Analysis (search analysis is made again when needed).

The image models

Three features use image models that run on this Mac but aren't built into the app, because they're large: Generate (in Extend, Remove Objects and Generate a Background) uses Stable Diffusion 1.5 Inpainting, about 2 GB; Colorize uses DDColor, about 425 MB; and Remove Objects' Erase uses LaMa, about 190 MB. They need Apple silicon.

  • Getting them. The first time you use the feature, the app offers to download its model; or click Download in Settings › Editing. Progress shows there and at the top of the window, and you can keep working meanwhile. Each download is checked against a fingerprint built into the app before it's installed.
  • Licenses. Stable Diffusion's license, CreativeML Open RAIL-M, limits how the model may be used (no illegal or harmful content, no deceiving or harassing people, among others), so the app asks you to accept it before downloading; View License shows it. DDColor's and LaMa's are Apache 2.0.
  • Where they live: ~/Library/Application Support/com.oneopa.AOK-ImageTool/Models. Remove… in Settings deletes one; you can download it again any time.
  • Memory. While you use Generate its model takes about 2.5 GB of memory; it's let go after a few idle minutes. The first use after installing takes a few seconds longer while macOS prepares the model for this Mac.
  • Making them yourself. The project's scripts/convert-inpainting-model.sh, scripts/convert-colorize-model.sh and scripts/convert-removal-model.sh convert the official weights; More › Install from Folder… in Settings installs the result.

Updates

  • AOK-ImageTool › Check for Updates… looks for a newer version. The app also checks once a day by itself (turn that off in Settings › General).
  • Installing an update downloads it, checks that it's AOK-ImageTool, signed by its developer and notarized by Apple, replaces the app and relaunches it. Unsaved edits are kept.
  • Help › Release Notes lists what changed in each version.

Privacy

Your pictures never leave your Mac. Descriptions, questions, search analysis, background removal, upscaling, colorizing, generated fills and backgrounds all run on this Mac. The app goes online only to download the generative fill and colorizing models when you ask for them, and to check GitHub for updates (once a day, unless you turn it off); macOS downloads Apple's upscaling model itself, the first time you use it. Search analysis is kept in ~/Library/Caches/com.oneopa.AOK-ImageTool; unsaved edits, backgrounds you choose and Extend fills in ~/Library/Application Support/com.oneopa.AOK-ImageTool.
